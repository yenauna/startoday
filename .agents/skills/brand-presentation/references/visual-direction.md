# Visual Direction — Behance Case-Study Style

## The core technique

A Behance-style brand case study is NOT a dashboard with bordered cards and labeled sections. It's a single long page where:

- **Each section takes its full width/height** and flows directly into the next, with no visible card borders separating them.
- **Backgrounds and color blocks ARE the section transitions** — a section introducing the palette might literally become a strip of the palette's colors, rather than a white card with a "Colors" heading on it.
- **Images and mockups are shown large** — close to full-bleed, not thumbnail-sized in a grid.
- **Typography carries weight as a design element itself**, not just as labels — a big, confident headline for the brand name is part of the visual impact, not just text.

These are compositional options, not requirements for every brand. A purposeful inset, image frame, or small card can help explain a real asset when the brand's visual language calls for it; avoid turning the entire page into a generic grid of UI cards.

## This page's own design must reflect the BRAND being presented, not a fixed template

The layout, spacing, and "energy" of the presentation page should change per brand, driven by the personality locked in `../brand-identity`:

- A **minimalist premium brand** → generous whitespace, restrained type, slow pacing between sections.
- A **playful/youthful brand** → tighter spacing allowed, more visual density, bolder color blocking.
- A **warm/craftsman brand** → textures/warmth in the background treatment (subtle, e.g. a soft off-white instead of stark white), rounded or hand-feel typography choices.

Never reuse the exact same layout/energy across two different brands' presentation pages — that is the single clearest sign the page was templated rather than designed for this brand.

## Translate references into layout decisions

Use `../../shared-references/brand-design-reference-library.md` and, when Arabic-market work is relevant, `../../shared-references/arabic-brand-design-reference-library.md` for credited examples. For instance, the public ONDO case emphasizes editorial negative space and tactile object views; MØD uses one symbol idea across packaging and experience; Lime Tiger uses a more energetic modular system; Summer 24 layers custom Arabic calligraphy and color transparency. These are distinct methods, not page templates. Select a method only when the locked brief supports it, then make the layout, color values, images, and story specific to the new brand.

Before coding, write a one-line art direction statement and a section map. The art direction statement should name actual page behaviors—such as dense/sparse rhythm, image crop, grid, background transitions, type scale, edge treatment—not just “premium” or “modern.”

## Checklist before shipping

- Does scrolling through this page feel like one coherent identity, or like six separate unrelated widgets stacked up?
- Would a real design client recognize their brand's actual personality in how this page itself feels, not just in its content?
- Are the mockups shown large enough to actually evaluate, or are they thumbnail-sized filler?
- Does the opening establish a specific identity idea, not just repeat the brand name?
- Does each transition earn its place and move the story forward?
- Are script direction, reading order, and type sizes comfortable on mobile?
