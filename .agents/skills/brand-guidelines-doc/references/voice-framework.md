# Brand Voice Framework

Voice is the brand's consistent character in words. Tone changes with context. Derive both from the locked identity brief and approved examples; do not assign generic traits just to fill a template.

## Choose three practical traits

Use a small number of distinctive, audience-relevant traits. For each, define an observable behavior and a boundary:

| Trait | We sound like | We avoid | Writing behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| [trait] | [plain description] | [specific anti-trait] | [sentence-level action] |

Prefer useful contrasts such as “warm and direct; not overly familiar” over unexplained adjective lists.

## Tone by context

Keep the voice consistent while adapting urgency, warmth, detail, and formality to the user's situation.

## Language and script

Record the language(s), dialect/register, spelling and transliteration rules, punctuation, numerals, and whether each channel uses Arabic, Latin, or both. Ask before choosing a dialect or translating brand copy. Review any local cultural reference with a knowledgeable speaker from the target market.

For Arabic/bilingual writing, specify right-to-left handling, the treatment of embedded Latin terms, and whether both scripts carry equal hierarchy. Keep the original approved brand name exactly as supplied.

## Build original examples

Use a real product or service fact from the brief. If no claim is approved, mark sample copy as a draft and avoid asserting performance, price, ingredients, health outcomes, awards, or customer results. Write both a positive example and a near-miss that explains the boundary.

## Voice check

1. Does the copy sound like the approved personality?
2. Can the intended audience understand it quickly?
3. Is the tone appropriate to the moment?
4. Is every factual claim supported by approved information?
5. Are language, dialect, direction, and cultural context correct?
6. Could a competitor use the same sentence unchanged? If so, make it more specific to the real offer and proof.
