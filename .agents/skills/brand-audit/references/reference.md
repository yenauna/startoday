# Reference Instructions — `brand-audit`

Read this file, `../../shared-references/research-reference-standard.md`, `../../shared-references/subagent-collaboration-protocol.md`, the existing strategy/identity files, and `../../shared-references/brand-intake-interview.md` before auditing. The skill is a diagnostic that can be used by itself; an audit does not authorize a redesign.

## Ask scope questions first

Audit supplied materials read-only. Ask only missing decisions that change coverage: objective (refresh, consistency, growth, merger, launch); specific business/brand and market; intended audience; touchpoints and channels to include; time period; source materials/data available; who will use the findings; and whether the user wants a written report or a prompt for another agent. Use no more than 3–6 high-impact questions in one round. Wait for confirmation of audit scope before project-specific external research or file creation. Do not ask routine design preferences.

## Establish the comparison standard

Use the approved `brand strategy.md` as the strategic baseline and `brand identity.txt`/guidelines as the design and behavior baseline. If these disagree, record the contradiction and ask which source is authoritative. If no strategy exists, do not invent one: either limit the audit to descriptive consistency or offer to route to `brand-strategy` after approval. Mark policy, customer promise, legal claims, and visual rules as verified only when their owners/source establish them.

## Collect representative evidence

Create a scope matrix before evaluating:

| Area | Example evidence | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy | Approved platform, objectives, audience and proof | Is the intended position clear, evidenced, and consistently used? |
| Messaging | Website, product pages, sales/support scripts, campaign copy | Are promise, claims, voice, and exact facts consistent? |
| Visual identity | Logo variants, palette, typography, graphics, photography | Does the system behave as approved across scales, scripts, backgrounds, and contexts? |
| Customer journey | Discovery, evaluation, purchase/onboarding, use, support | Are identity and promise recognizable and deliverable at important moments? |
| Product/service | Actual offer, packaging/service steps, variants, feature claims | Does reality support what communications promise? |
| Governance | Owner, approvals, templates, versions, partner handoffs | Can teams use the system without inventing missing rules? |

Inspect a representative selection across the agreed scope, not only the brand's best portfolio images. Record source, date/access, what was directly observed, what is inferred, and what is missing. For visual claims, open and inspect actual images; a title, snippet, or text summary does not count. For external benchmarks that materially inform a finding, inspect at least five distinct relevant public projects/pages and apply the shared visual-analysis card where imagery matters. Use Agent Reach as allowed; stop at login, CAPTCHA, rate limits, and blocks.

## Write findings that a decision-maker can act on

Use one finding card per material issue:

```text
Finding:
Evidence / direct sources:
Approved strategy or rule involved:
Observed gap (separate from interpretation):
Who or what is affected:
Impact: critical / high / medium / low (explain basis)
Confidence: high / medium / low
Recommended action:
Suggested skill owner:
Open question / approval required:
```

Prioritize issues that weaken the approved promise, create false expectations, confuse audiences or scripts, fracture recognition, or prevent correct application. Keep subjective taste feedback separate. Don't assign a numeric score unless the user needs a repeatable comparison and the evidence supports one.

## Route and close

- Strategy conflict or positioning gap → `brand-strategy`.
- One proposed decision needs a fit judgment → `brand-compass`.
- Identity-system specification → `brand-identity`.
- Logo concept, usage, wordmark, color, or pattern → matching logo specialist.
- Product scene / campaign / presentation / written guide → matching application skill.

Present the scope, sample limitations, top findings, confidence, and next-step choices. Ask which action to approve. Do not silently change strategy, create/rewrite files, or start remediation. If an access limit leaves the evidence incomplete, state exactly what was not checked.
