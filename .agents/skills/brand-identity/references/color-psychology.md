# Color Psychology — Quick Reference

Use this to propose and explain palette directions from the approved audience, market, and personality. These associations are hypotheses, not universal meanings. Do not lock proposed hex values until the user approves them.

## Base associations

| Color family | Common association | Good for | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red/Tomato | Appetite, urgency, warmth, energy | Food, sales, bold brands | Overused in food — needs a twist to stand out |
| Orange | Friendly, affordable, energetic | Everyday/value brands, food delivery | Can read as "cheap" if overused |
| Yellow | Optimism, attention-grabbing | Accent only — rarely a primary | Fatigues the eye at large sizes |
| Brown/Caramel/Terracotta | Warm, grounded, artisanal, trustworthy-casual | Grocery, coffee, bakery, home goods | Can look dated without a sharp accent color |
| Green | Natural, fresh, health, growth, calm | Organic/health food, finance (dark green), sustainability | Overused in "eco" branding — needs distinctiveness |
| Blue | Trust, calm, professional, corporate | Tech, finance, healthcare, B2B | Can read as cold/impersonal without a warm accent |
| Purple | Premium, creative, a little mysterious | Beauty, luxury, creative services | Can skew "feminine-coded" by old stereotype — fine to ignore, but know the association exists |
| Black/near-black | Premium, serious, minimal | Luxury, fashion, tech | Flat and lifeless without a strong accent or texture |
| Cream/off-white | Soft, approachable, warm neutral | Almost any brand as a background instead of stark white | None significant |
| Pink | Playful, youthful, or premium depending on shade | Beauty, youth brands, dessert | Pale pink reads "soft/feminine"; hot pink reads "bold/youthful" — pick deliberately |

Color meaning changes by market, category, neighboring colors, material, and context. Confirm relevant cultural meaning with the target audience or a knowledgeable local reviewer when it matters.

## Building a 3–4 color palette

1. **Primary** — the color people will remember the brand by. Usually carries the main personality association above.
2. **Secondary** — a neutral (off-white, cream, deep brown, near-black) that does most of the actual page/background work. The primary should sit on top of this, not next to another loud color.
3. **Accent** — one higher-contrast color used sparingly (a crystal shape, a CTA button, a price tag) to draw the eye. Should be a genuine contrast to the primary/secondary, not a slightly different shade of the same hue.
4. **(Optional) Ink** — a text color selected for the approved palette and contrast needs. It need not be a particular warm/cool black or white; propose it only when the brief needs it and get it approved.

## Common mistake this reference exists to prevent

Picking colors that are individually attractive but do not map to the brand's actual audience, offer, or differentiation. State why each color supports the brief and what role it serves; if no reason comes to mind, keep it as a candidate rather than locking it.

## Approval and use checks

- Copy exact approved hex codes into every downstream image prompt; don't translate them into vague words like “warm red” or “soft green.”
- Check the actual text/background pairs and contrast. A palette does not pass accessibility as a whole; each pair used for text or controls must be checked.
- Test the palette in the materials and lighting that matter. A printed color on kraft, glass, or fabric may behave differently from a flat screen swatch.
- Record proposed, approved, and production-adjusted values separately. Never update the source palette silently to match an image-generation result.
