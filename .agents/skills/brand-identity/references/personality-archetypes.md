# Personality Archetypes

A shorthand vocabulary for brand personality — pick one primary and optionally one secondary. Each archetype below lists the visual/verbal tendencies it implies, so picking one archetype effectively pre-answers a dozen later design questions at once.

| Archetype | Feels like | Typical colors | Typical type | Typical logo style | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Everyday Friend / Warm & Familiar** | A trusted local shop, a neighbor | Warm earth tones, cream, terracotta, muted red | Rounded sans or soft serif | Simple flat icon, hand-feel, approachable | Cold corporate blues, sharp angular marks |
| **The Modern Minimalist** | Clean, confident, understated | 1–2 colors max, lots of white/neutral space | Clean geometric sans | Single abstract mark, generous negative space | Clutter, gradients, more than 2 fonts |
| **The Bold Disruptor** | Loud, young, confident, breaks category norms | High-contrast, saturated, often black + one neon/bright | Heavy display type, oversized | Graphic, high-contrast, sometimes typographic-only | Pastels, delicate linework, symmetry-for-its-own-sake |
| **The Premium / Luxury** | Perceived value, confidence, refinement, or exclusivity as defined by the brief | No default palette; choose approved colors for the audience and tier (dark, jewel, metallic, and monochrome options are not automatic) | The approved type system that best signals the intended value and works in every required script | A deliberate, recognizable mark and controlled detail only if supported by the positioning | Generic luxury clichés, unreadable restraint, unsupported claims, and any cue that conflicts with audience or channel |
| **The Playful / Youthful** | Fun, energetic, a little chaotic on purpose | Bright multi-color palettes, high saturation | Rounded, bouncy, sometimes hand-drawn | Mascot or illustrated character system, stickers | Muted/serious tones, formal serif type |
| **The Craftsman / Artisanal** | Handmade, slow, careful, traditional | Muted naturals, kraft brown, deep greens | Serif or slab serif, sometimes a custom/hand-lettered mark | Emblem/badge style, stamp-like, textured | Glossy gradients, futuristic/tech visuals |
| **The Trustworthy Institution** | Established, reliable, safe | Navy, deep green, burgundy, gold accents | Classic serif or sturdy sans | Shield/crest-like or solid wordmark | Trendy effects, neon, anything that looks temporary |
| **The Tech-Forward** | Smart, efficient, slightly futuristic | Deep blues/purples/black with one bright accent (often a gradient) | Clean geometric sans, sometimes monospace accents | Abstract geometric mark, can use gradients | Hand-drawn textures, kraft/organic materials |

## How to use this with a real brief

1. Use the intake interview to understand the audience, market, offer, story, and constraints before proposing a personality route.
2. Treat a selected archetype as a hypothesis. Its color, typography, and logo tendencies are conversation starters only; propose options and get approval before adding them to the brief or any prompt.
3. A brand can blend two archetypes (e.g. "Everyday Friend" + a touch of "Craftsman" for a bakery), but never more than two unless the user has a clear reason; blending too many can make the direction hard to express consistently.

## Use archetypes as hypotheses, not recipes

The listed colors and logo tendencies are conversation starters, not defaults or rules. Validate them against the actual audience, market, category conventions, competitive set, script needs, and production context. A warm brand does not automatically need brown; a trustworthy service does not automatically need navy.

Translate each selected trait into observable design behavior before prompting. For example, “confident” might mean a strong headline scale and a simple mark, while “welcoming” might mean generous spacing and human-scale imagery. The visual choices should be stated in the brief and approved before they become downstream prompt instructions.

When the user requests reference research, use the [general library](../../shared-references/brand-design-reference-library.md) and, for Arabic-market work, the [Arabic-market library](../../shared-references/arabic-brand-design-reference-library.md) to compare different ways the same broad personality can be expressed. Keep the brief's own direction distinct from any reference's exact color, typeface, shape, or layout.
