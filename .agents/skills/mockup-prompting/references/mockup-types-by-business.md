# Mockup Types by Business Category

Pick from the relevant row — don't generate a generic universal set for every brand.

## Small retail / grocery / corner shop
- Shopping bag (paper and/or plastic)
- Storefront sign / shop awning
- Shopping basket or cart (label/sticker on it)
- Street advertising board / weekly-offers standee
- Price tags / shelf labels
- Receipt header (if relevant)

## Cafe / restaurant / food & beverage
- Cup/packaging (coffee cup, takeaway box)
- Menu board or printed menu
- Napkin or paper bag
- Storefront signage
- Staff apron (only if the brand genuinely has front-facing staff in branded wear)
- Delivery packaging (if relevant)

## Clothing / apparel brand
- T-shirt / hoodie flat-lay or on-model studio shot
- Garment tag / label
- Packaging (poly mailer, box)
- Shopping bag
- Lookbook-style photography (only for marketing, not for the identity presentation page)

## Tech app / SaaS / digital product
- App icon on a phone home screen
- UI screen mockup (login, dashboard)
- Browser tab / favicon
- Social media profile picture context
- Laptop/device mockup with the product on screen
- (Rarely needs physical-world mockups like bags or signage at all)

## Professional services / B2B / agency
- Business card
- Letterhead / document header
- Email signature
- Website hero section
- Presentation/deck cover slide
- Office signage (if the business has a physical office)

## Beauty / cosmetics / wellness
- Hero product's primary package (bottle, jar, tube, compact, or applicator, only as confirmed)
- Matching secondary carton or approved product set, showing the shared label grid and hierarchy
- One coherent approved SKU family with only the user's confirmed variants and the locked variant-coding rule
- Product listing/e-commerce hero or retail shelf/counter display only when that channel is in scope
- Shipping or gifting packaging only if the business offers it
- The actual uploaded logo and palette/design-system reference must be assigned to the image model as reference inputs when available; do not produce a generic unbranded “beauty” package as the final mockup

Ask what “premium” means for this audience and product tier. Translate the answer into approved typography, hierarchy, imagery, and feasible material/finish cues; do not default to black, gold, glass, marble, metallic foil, or generic spa props. Keep package structure, ingredients, product benefits, size, variants, and label copy to supplied/approved facts. A visual mockup does not confirm manufacturability or regulatory compliance.

## Hospitality / hotel / travel
- Key card, welcome letter, amenity kit, room-service menu
- Wayfinding, door sign, reception signage, or exterior sign when the property is in scope
- Website booking/arrival screen or guest app when digital touchpoints matter
- Gift shop or food packaging only if the hotel sells them

## Floral / gifts / specialty retail
- Wrap, ribbon, tag, card, delivery sleeve, or reusable bag
- Product arrangement and packaging shown together at realistic scale
- Shop sign or online shop listing when these are actual customer touchpoints

## Healthcare / clinic / pharmacy
- Service identity on appointment card, wayfinding, clinic reception, or patient-facing digital flow
- Product packaging or instruction materials only where the brand owns those touchpoints
- Any medication or health claims require user-supplied approved copy; never invent them

## Education / event / cultural organization
- Event poster, program, ticket, directional signage, stage/backdrop, and social announcement
- Bilingual or multi-script applications when required by the audience and market
- Merchandise only when the organization genuinely sells or distributes it

## Consumer goods / packaged food
- A small product family with the approved variant-coding rule
- Primary package, shipping carton, shelf placement, and delivery context as relevant
- Keep regulatory, ingredient, nutrition, certification, and price fields blank unless exact approved copy is supplied

## Events / entertainment
- Poster / flyer
- Ticket design
- Banner / step-and-repeat backdrop
- Social media promotional graphic (hand this to `../banner-design` instead)

## General rule for anything not listed

Ask: "where does a real customer actually SEE this brand?" List those real touchpoints first, then generate mockups only for those — not for every possible surface a logo could theoretically go on.

## Prioritize a small set

Score candidate touchpoints informally against these questions, then choose the few that tell the strongest story:

1. **Visibility:** Is this where the audience encounters or uses the brand?
2. **Identity proof:** Does this application test an important logo, type, color, image, or pattern behavior?
3. **Business fit:** Does the brand really offer this product/service or own this environment?
4. **Variation:** Does the set show a different design challenge rather than repeating the same object?
5. **Feasibility:** Can the mockup plausibly represent the right material, crop, market, and content?

Use a real competitor/application reference only to identify common conventions and opportunities to differentiate. Do not reproduce a competitor's packaging, mark, or signature graphics.
