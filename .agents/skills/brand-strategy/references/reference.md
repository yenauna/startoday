# Reference Instructions — `brand-strategy`

Read this file, `../../shared-references/research-reference-standard.md`, `../../shared-references/subagent-collaboration-protocol.md`, and `../../shared-references/brand-intake-interview.md` before strategic work. A standalone request may begin and end here; do not force the complete design pipeline.

## Ask first, but only about strategic pivots

Audit supplied business/brand files read-only. Ask a compact set of no more than 3–6 missing questions that could change the strategy: business stage and goal; real offer and economics at a high level; priority audience and context; country/category; customer problem or unmet need; competitive alternatives; defensible strengths and proof; portfolio/sub-brand complexity; constraints; and which decisions the strategy should guide. Do not ask for colors, fonts, logos, channels, or campaign formats unless they affect the stated strategic choice. If the brief is already locked, reuse it. Wait for approval of the research question and strategy scope before project-specific web research or writing.

## Research sources and evidence

For a new strategy or repositioning, examine at least five distinct, relevant brands or public case studies in the target category/market, plus credible category/customer evidence when available. Prefer official company materials, annual or impact reports, product/service pages, customer research the user supplied, reputable industry sources, and credited case studies. Use Google Images, Behance, or design publications to discover useful identity work, then open the canonical project/company source. Inspect actual brand applications where useful; an image can show positioning cues but cannot establish commercial performance or customer perception by itself.

At the start of the research pass, follow the Agent Reach skill's public-access rules. Native public search/read tools may be used first; use Agent Reach on channels it supports when native access is missing or incomplete. It is read-only and may return text without image pixels. Use a visual-capable viewer for actual images, do not claim an image was inspected unless visible, and do not bypass login, CAPTCHA, blocks, or rate limits. If direct image retrieval is permitted and needed, use only a small task-local set and do not retain or redistribute artwork.

Record per source: canonical URL and publisher/creator; date/status; target market; what the source explicitly says; what was observed in actual messaging/identity/customer-facing work; what is inferred; a transferable strategic lesson; and what is not proven. For the user's category, compare audience, category frame, offer, promise, proof, differentiators, business model signals, naming/portfolio structure, message hierarchy, and visible identity/experience only where evidence exists. Mark concept work, self-published portfolios, and uncertain status accurately.

## Strategy construction sequence

Use this chain and leave unsupported links open:

1. **Business context:** what the organization offers, to whom, in which markets, at what stage, and what outcome it needs.
2. **Audience choice:** priority segment or situation, customer need/job, barriers, and evidence quality. Separate buyer, user, influencer, and decision maker when they differ.
3. **Category and alternatives:** the frame customers use, direct competitors, substitutes, current behaviors, and why the chosen frame matters.
4. **Opportunity/insight:** a specific tension or unmet need supported by evidence—not a universal human truth dressed as research.
5. **Differentiated value:** what the brand does uniquely or credibly better, why it matters, and which capabilities prove it.
6. **Promise and proof:** one meaningful promise and the evidence, product behavior, service practice, or capability that supports it. Keep aspirational proof marked as a plan, not a current fact.
7. **Positioning:** for [priority audience] in [category/context], [brand] is [distinct value/frame] because [credible proof], unlike [relevant alternatives]. This is a working statement, not necessarily public copy.
8. **Strategic pillars and trade-offs:** a small number of decisions that guide offer, experience, voice, and identity; explicitly name audiences, opportunities, behaviors, or claims to decline.
9. **Architecture (only if needed):** show the role and relationship of parent, masterbrand, sub-brand, product, and endorsed names; do not invent a portfolio structure for a single-offer brand.
10. **Strategic implications and sequence:** identify which offer, service, experience, identity, messaging, or internal behaviors should change first, what depends on what, and which decisions remain with the owner. Keep this a brand-building roadmap, not a media plan.
11. **Success signals:** define observable brand/business indicators with a source or owner; do not promise causal lift from brand work alone.

## Deliverable and handoff

When a strategy document is requested, organize `brand strategy.md` around: approved business objective; audience; category; need/insight and evidence; positioning; value/promise; proof; pillars; voice/behavioral principles; architecture if applicable; trade-offs/avoidances; priority implications and dependencies; success signals; source log; assumptions; approvals/version. Distinguish **approved fact**, **evidence**, **interpretation**, **proposal**, and **open decision**.

The handoff to `brand-identity` is a concise approved block: name/scripts; offer/category; priority audiences/market; position and promise; proof and prohibited claims; story facts; differentiators; personality/behavior; portfolio structure; must-keep/must-avoid; open questions. `brand-identity` owns the visual identity specification. If the strategy is not approved, downstream design must remain provisional.

Do not attach competitor/reference artwork as an imitation target. Do not recommend a strategy because a portfolio case looks attractive. If fewer than five relevant cases or source evidence are accessible, say so, identify what was missing, and keep the recommendation provisional rather than inventing certainty.
