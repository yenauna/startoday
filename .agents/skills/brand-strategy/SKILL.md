# Brand Strategy — Build the Strategic Foundation

Use this skill to decide what the brand should mean, whom it should matter to, and what choices make it distinct and credible. This skill can be invoked by itself; it does not require `lets-brand`. In the complete workflow, `lets-brand` calls it before `brand-identity` when strategy is missing or changing.

## Read first

Read `references/reference.md`, `../shared-references/research-reference-standard.md`, `../shared-references/subagent-collaboration-protocol.md`, and `../shared-references/brand-intake-interview.md`. If `brand strategy.md` or `brand identity.txt` exists, inspect and preserve it. Research is not approval: ask only missing, consequential questions and wait for scope approval before project-specific research or writing.

## When to use

- A new business or brand needs a strategic foundation before visual identity.
- A live brand needs a repositioning, sharper differentiation, or revised promise.
- The user needs to define the target audience, category frame, value proposition, brand architecture, or reasons to believe.
- A design brief has attractive adjectives but no clear customer, business, or positioning logic.

For a review of one proposed action against an approved strategy, use `../brand-compass/SKILL.md`. For a broad diagnosis of an existing brand's consistency and gaps, use `../brand-audit/SKILL.md`. For visual system decisions, use `../brand-identity/SKILL.md` after strategic approval.

## Workflow

1. **Audit the business and prior decisions.** Inspect existing strategy and identity documents, product/service facts, owner-approved claims, customer evidence, market notes, and prior approvals. Separate confirmed evidence, assumptions, hypotheses, and unknowns.
2. **Interview for strategic pivots.** Ask no more than 3–6 high-impact questions at a time. Focus on business stage and objective; offer; priority customer and unmet need; geography/category; alternatives and competitors; distinctive capabilities/proof; growth or portfolio scope; constraints; and the decisions the strategy must guide. Skip facts already supplied.
3. **Lock scope before research.** Summarize the question to solve, market, audience, evidence needed, and requested output. Wait for approval unless the user already authorized that exact scope. Do not use easy execution preferences as a reason to delay.
4. **Research the category and evidence.** Read the strategy-specific reference. Use primary and credible sources for market facts. Inspect relevant brand cases and actual customer-facing identity/experience images; distinguish the brand's stated positioning from what the evidence supports. Follow the Agent Reach access rules and report source limits.
5. **Build a strategic platform.** Synthesize the category frame, priority audience and need, problem or opportunity, differentiated value, promise, reasons to believe, positioning, strategic pillars, personality/behavioral principles, trade-offs, and what the brand will not claim or pursue. Include architecture only when the offer or portfolio calls for it. Translate the strategy into practical priorities across offer, service, customer experience, identity, messaging, and feedback; do not turn this into media buying or paid-channel operations.
6. **Compare viable routes.** If evidence supports materially different positions, present up to three distinct options with audience, value, proof, trade-offs, and risks. Recommend one with a reason, but leave the choice to the user. Do not disguise a preference as research fact.
7. **Confirm and deliver.** Show a concise strategy lock and wait for approval before treating it as authoritative. Save `brand strategy.md` only when the user requested a strategy artifact or the approved `lets-brand` workflow requires it. Preserve existing files and update only approved sections.
8. **Hand off.** Give `brand-identity` the approved strategy snapshot and open visual-system decisions. Give `brand-compass` the approved principles and decision tests. Give `brand-audit` the strategy baseline and evidence standard. Do not silently start identity, logo, campaign, or media execution.

## Hard boundaries

- Do not invent customer research, market size, competitive advantage, founder story, customer quote, performance result, or proof.
- Do not define a visual identity or produce logo, poster, mockup, HTML, or image-generation prompts as a substitute for strategy.
- Do not turn a strategy exercise into media buying, paid-channel budgets, campaign targeting, ad bidding, or media operations.
- Mark recommendations as proposed until the user approves them. Treat the strategy document as the source of truth only after approval.
- Keep the output useful: a decision system, not a pile of slogans, archetypes, or generic mission statements.
